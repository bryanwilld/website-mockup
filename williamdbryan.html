About

Scholar, author, and policy researcher. I study how the past shapes the present — and use that work to benefit people and nature.

A single question has animated my research across two books and more than a decade of policy work: can private sector actors solve our most pressing environmental problems, and when do their solutions fall short? It’s a question I first encountered in the historical record and have spent my career testing against the present.

My first book, The Price of Permanence: Nature and Business in the New South (University of Georgia Press, 2018), examined the evolution of business-led conservation policies in the post-Civil War South. These policies were successful in maintaining stocks of profitable natural resources — but they also underpinned the exploitative racial status quo by privatizing common land and resources. The lesson: without the backstop of regulation, even ambitious private sector efforts fall short of what communities actually need.

My second book, under contract with the University of North Carolina Press, extends this inquiry into the twentieth century — exploring how effective private residential developers have been in shaping the built environment in sustainable ways through law and planning, including along the coast, and where the limits of market-based solutions lie.

For the past several years I’ve pursued these questions in an applied setting as Research Director at the Southeast Energy Efficiency Alliance in Atlanta, where I lead research on housing, climate, and energy. The work is data-driven and policy-focused, but it’s grounded in the same historical conviction: that you can’t fix a problem you don’t understand, and understanding it requires knowing how it got there.

Across all of this work, I’m drawn to places where public and private interests collide — coastlines, public lands, energy infrastructure, housing markets. These are the places where the question of who benefits and who bears the cost is most visible, and most consequential.

I write for general audiences, speak at universities and policy conferences, and hold a Ph.D. in Environmental History from Penn State and a B.A. from Furman University. I live and work in Atlanta, Georgia.

Books & Writing

Scholarship that travels beyond the academy.

My scholarly work asks how ideas about nature, resources, and sustainability have shaped American history — and what that history reveals about the limits of market-based solutions to environmental problems. I also write for policy and public audiences, because the research matters most when it reaches people who can use it.

University of Georgia Press · 2018
The Price of Permanence: Nature and Business in the New South
A reinterpretation of the post-Civil War South through an environmental lens. Business leaders promoted conservation not despite economic ambition but because of it — and the ideology of “permanence” shaped both the national conservation movement and the logic of Jim Crow. The first work to write the South into the national conservation movement, and to show how resource policy fueled racial hierarchy.

Research & Policy

From the archive to the policy table.

My research spans academic scholarship and applied policy work — connected by a common conviction that rigorous inquiry should drive real change. Across both, I’m interested in how decisions about land, energy, housing, and water shape opportunity, and who bears the cost when those decisions go wrong.

Academic scholarship

Two books with major university presses. Seven peer-reviewed articles in leading journals. Book chapters on environmental, policy, and economic history. Contributions to the Fifth National Climate Assessment.

Applied policy research

14+ policy reports on energy insecurity, healthy housing, building codes, and coastal development. Partnerships with environmental NGOs, state and local governments, federal agencies, and national laboratories. Research that has directed more than $100M to underserved communities.

Public education

Public seminars at Emory University and through Georgia Humanities. 75+ invited talks at universities, policy conferences, and national parks. Op-eds in the Washington Post and regional outlets.

Digital projects
